Abstract

Accumulating LiDAR scans can retain incompatible surfaces from different times, even when sensor poses are fixed. In dynamic scenes, simply removing these surfaces also removes the objects that a time-resolved reconstruction should preserve. We present Temporal Evidence Fusion (TEF), a surface-fusion method organized around this distinction. For the background, TEF aggregates observations into temporal blocks, limits each block's fusion weight, and uses surface-hit and free-space traversal evidence to revise a normalized signed-distance field. An object-centric extension maintains local object geometry and composes it with causally estimated poses to produce timestamped scene meshes. It separates observation delivery, pose acceptance, and shape integration, with optional image-based gradient refinement of object pose. Across four fixed-input segments from Newer College and Oxford Spires, TEF obtains higher F-scores at 10 cm than the tested PIN-SLAM and VDBFusion configurations. A same-code ablation reduces local multilayer intersections from 27.92% to 17.52%, while reducing coverage from 48.29% to 42.09%. A KITTI object-reconstruction case study achieves a 6.50 cm median center error with LiDAR-only updates; image refinement has mixed effects. These results support temporal evidence for suppressing accumulated layers and demonstrate an object-local 4D integration path, without establishing general dynamic-reconstruction superiority or end-to-end real-time operation.

All three sequences use the dynamic extension in LiDAR + image mode, with cached point ownership and fixed sensor poses. KITTI 0059 (target 51, frames 180–279) is the paper's case study; its pose errors come from a separate post-run audit. KITTI 0005 and 0014 are display runs of the same code on saved object tracks: they are not part of the paper's evaluation and were not compared with annotations. The image step needs enough tracked features on the vehicle mask; it was accepted on 82 of 89 attempts for the 0059 car and 38 of 48 for the 0005 van, while for the cyclist and most oncoming cars it found too few features and the pose came from LiDAR registration alone. Each object mesh is revised every 5 frames and shown from the frame it becomes available. What the temporal evidence changes

Same-code 2×2 ablation on the NCD development segment: bounded versus summed block weights, and traversal (pass) evidence on or off. Multilayer rate is measured on 11 295 wall cross-sections covered by all four arms; coverage on all 28 042; ray categories on 600 000 held-out rays. The mechanism suppresses accumulated layers and free-space conflicts at a measurable coverage cost.
